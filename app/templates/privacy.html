Privacy and your data

What this app reads from your mailbox, what it keeps, and who can see it. Last updated 24 September 2026.

The short version

  • The app has read-only access. It cannot send, delete, move or change your email.
  • Yes, it stores the text of your emails in its database so the dashboard is fast. The subject, message text and AI summary are encrypted there, so a copy of the database or a backup is unreadable without the app's key.
  • But the running app holds that key, so whoever runs the server can still read your mail. The sender's address, dates, priority and short task titles are not encrypted.
  • Emails ranked urgent or important are sent to Google's Gemini AI to write the summary, if the person running the app has set that up.
  • Disconnecting a mailbox deletes its stored mail. There are no ads, no trackers and nothing is sold.

What the app can read

  • Gmail: the gmail.readonly permission. Outlook / Microsoft 365: Mail.Read, User.Read and offline_access (so it can keep syncing without you signing in each time).
  • It looks at recent mail (the last day, week or month you choose) and, for the Trips tab, searches up to 180 days back for ticket and booking confirmations.

What is stored

  • For each email: sender, subject, the message text (the first 4,000 characters; up to 20,000 for ticket and booking mails), the date, the priority the app gave it, and the AI summary if one was made.
  • Encrypted in the database: the subject, the message text, the AI summary and the notes on calendar items. Readable in the database: the sender address, the date, the priority, the short task title (the first words of a subject) and calendar item titles, because the app needs to sort and group by them.
  • Derived from your mail: tasks and deadlines, and the trips and tickets shown on the Trips tab (flights, trains, movies and so on).
  • Your account: your email address, a hashed password (never the password itself), the mailboxes you connected, and the calendar items you or the app created.
  • Your access tokens for Gmail / Outlook are stored encrypted.
  • Not stored: attachments, images, or the original HTML of messages.

Where it is stored, and who can see it

  • In the app's database on the server where it runs (a local file, or a PostgreSQL database). The subject, text and summary of each email, and your access tokens, are encrypted with the server's encryption key (Fernet: AES-128 with an integrity check). That protects database files, dumps and backups.
  • It does not protect against someone who can run or log in to the live server: the app decrypts mail to show it to you, so the key is there too. Because stored text is encrypted, even the leftover bytes a database keeps after something is deleted are unreadable. Mail stored before encryption was switched on stays readable until the operator converts it (which also compacts the database) and deletes old backups. If the key is lost, stored mail cannot be read again (syncing fetches it afresh, but mailboxes must be reconnected).
  • Google Gemini: the text of urgent and important emails (up to 4,000 characters each) is sent to Google's Gemini API to create summaries. If no Gemini key is configured, nothing is sent. How Google handles that data depends on the API plan the operator uses, so check Google's terms if that matters to you.
  • Gmail and Microsoft see the app connecting to your account, as they do for any app you authorise.
  • Your browser loads fonts from Google Fonts and styling from the Tailwind CDN. Those services can see your IP address and browser type, but never your email.
  • The app does not use your mail to train anything, has no analytics or advertising, and does not sell or share your data.

On your device

  • Your settings (theme, accent colour, dashboard choices) are saved in this browser's local storage. They never include mail.
  • A signed session cookie keeps you signed in.
  • Your calendar link is a secret address: anyone who has it can see your scheduled items. You can create a new one at any time.

Your controls

  • Disconnect a mailbox (the × next to it in the menu). This deletes the mail stored from it, its sync history and the tasks created from those emails, and asks Google to revoke the access token. For Outlook, also remove the app at account.microsoft.com/privacy/app-access. For Google you can check myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete any calendar item yourself from the Activity tab.
  • Not available yet: a one-click "delete my whole account" and an automatic limit on how long mail is kept. To have your account and data removed today, ask the person who runs this app.
